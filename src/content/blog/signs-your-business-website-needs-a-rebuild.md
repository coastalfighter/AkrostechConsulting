Your website is often the first conversation a prospect has with your business. When it is slow, confusing or insecure, people leave before they ever contact you.

Here are seven signs it is time for a rebuild rather than another patch.

## 1. It is slow on a phone

Most visitors arrive on mobile. If your pages take more than a few seconds to load, many of them never see your content. Run your homepage through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). If your mobile performance score is in the red, speed is costing you leads.

## 2. It was not built for small screens

Pinch-to-zoom text, buttons too close together and menus that do not open are signs of a site designed for desktop first. A modern build is designed mobile-first and tested on real devices.

## 3. Your team avoids updating it

If changing a headline needs a developer, your content goes stale. A good content management system lets your team edit pages, publish posts and update services without touching code.

## 4. Security warnings or strange content appear

Outdated plugins and themes are the most common way small-business sites get compromised. Signs include spam pages you did not create, unexpected redirects, or links to unrelated sites appearing in your content.

> If you see content on your site that you did not publish, treat it as a security incident: change passwords, update everything and get the site cleaned before anything else.

## 5. It does not reflect what you sell today

Services change. If your site still describes what you offered two years ago, visitors get the wrong picture, and search engines rank you for the wrong things.

## 6. It is hard to use with a keyboard or screen reader

Accessibility is good for everyone and is increasingly expected. Low-contrast text, images without descriptions and forms without labels shut people out. Modern builds target the WCAG 2.2 AA standard.

## 7. It does not turn visitors into leads

A beautiful site that does not generate enquiries is a brochure. Every page should make the next step obvious: book a call, request a quote or get in touch.

## Rebuild or refresh?

If you recognise one or two signs, a focused refresh may be enough. If you recognise four or more, a rebuild usually costs less over time than continual patching.
