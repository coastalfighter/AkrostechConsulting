Choose **Shopify** when selling products online is the core of the business. Choose **WordPress** when you want a familiar editor, a huge plugin ecosystem and a content-led site your team can manage, and you are willing to keep it updated. Choose **Next.js with a headless CMS** when performance, custom design and integrations matter most and you have, or can hire, developers to own it. Each is a sound platform; the right one depends on what the site has to do and who will run it.

Here is how they compare on the factors that matter after launch.

## What is the difference between the three?

- **WordPress** is a content management system that powers a large share of the web. You pick or build a theme, add plugins for features and edit content in a built-in dashboard. You manage hosting and updates.
- **Shopify** is a hosted commerce platform. Hosting, checkout, payments and platform security are handled for you. You customize a theme and add apps for extra features.
- **Next.js** is a React framework for building fast, custom websites and web apps. It is usually paired with a headless CMS, such as Sanity or Contentful, so editors manage content separately from the code.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Next.js + headless CMS | WordPress | Shopify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Custom marketing sites, web apps, integrations | Content-led sites, blogs, broad plugin needs | Online stores |
| Content editing | Structured CMS, very flexible once set up | Familiar dashboard and block editor | Product and page editing built in |
| Performance | Excellent when built well | Varies widely by theme and plugins | Good, depends on theme and apps |
| Design freedom | Full | High with custom theme, limited with off-the-shelf | Theme-based with customization |
| E-commerce | Possible via headless commerce or Shopify integration | WooCommerce | Native and mature |
| Security responsibility | Small attack surface; dependencies need updates | Core, theme and plugin updates are essential | Platform managed by Shopify |
| Hosting | Vercel, AWS or similar | Any PHP host or managed WordPress host | Included |
| Developer needed for changes | For new templates or features | Often not, for content and simple changes | Often not, for content and products |
| Ongoing costs to consider | Hosting, CMS plan, developer time | Hosting, premium plugins, maintenance | Subscription, apps, transaction fees |

## When does WordPress make the most sense?

WordPress is a good fit when:

- Your team publishes a lot of content and wants a familiar editor
- You need features that already exist as well-supported plugins
- You want a wide pool of developers and agencies to choose from
- You are committed to regular updates, or you will buy a care plan

The main risk is maintenance. Outdated plugins and themes are a common way sites get compromised. A WordPress site needs core, theme and plugin updates, backups and monitoring. Keep plugins to the ones you actually use, because each one adds code and potential vulnerabilities.

## When does Shopify make the most sense?

Shopify is a good fit when:

- Selling products is the primary job of the site
- You want checkout, payments and platform security handled for you
- Your team needs to manage products, orders and inventory without a developer
- You expect to use apps for reviews, subscriptions, shipping and marketing

Watch the app count. Each app can add scripts to your storefront, which affects speed. Review apps periodically and remove anything not earning its place.

If you need a store and a content-heavy marketing site, consider WooCommerce on WordPress, or a Next.js front end connected to Shopify for checkout.

## When does Next.js make the most sense?

Next.js with a headless CMS is a good fit when:

- Site speed and Core Web Vitals are a priority, for example because you run paid traffic
- You want a fully custom design and a reusable component system
- The site needs integrations with a CRM, booking system, internal API or customer portal
- You may grow the site into a web app or dashboard
- You have, or will hire, developers to own the code

The trade-off is that new templates and features need a developer. Content editing is straightforward once the CMS is set up, but the initial setup takes careful planning. Our guide to [Core Web Vitals for business owners](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained-for-business-owners) explains why performance can be worth that investment.

## Five questions to make the decision

1. **Is the main goal selling products?** If yes, start with Shopify.
2. **Who will edit the site day to day,** and how comfortable are they with tools?
3. **Which integrations are required** now and in the next two years?
4. **How important is page speed** to your marketing and conversion goals?
5. **Who will maintain the site** after launch: your team, a developer or a care plan?

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a B2B software company runs paid campaigns to service and landing pages and wants deep CRM integration and a customer resources hub. It chooses Next.js with a headless CMS so marketing can publish landing pages from reusable components. A regional gift retailer, by contrast, chooses Shopify so its small team can manage products, orders and promotions without a developer. A consulting firm with a large article library and modest feature needs stays on WordPress with a hardened, lightweight theme and a care plan.

## What about migrating platforms?

Migrations are common and manageable with a plan:

- Inventory every URL and map each one to its new address with redirects
- Move content and metadata, including titles and descriptions
- Rebuild forms and integrations and test them end to end
- Set up analytics and search console before launch
- Monitor rankings, traffic and errors in the weeks after launch

For help budgeting a migration, see [how much a business website costs and what drives the price](/blog/how-much-does-a-business-website-cost).

## Key takeaways

- Shopify is the simplest path when selling products is the core job of the site.
- WordPress suits content-led sites with familiar editing, provided it is kept updated.
- Next.js with a headless CMS suits custom, high-performance sites with complex integrations.
- Every platform's performance depends on how it is built and how many plugins, apps or scripts you add.
- Decide based on goals, editors, integrations, speed and who will maintain the site.
