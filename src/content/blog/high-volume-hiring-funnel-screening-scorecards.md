To hire at high volume without lowering the bar, you need three things working together: a funnel with clearly defined stages and pass criteria, a screening scorecard that every screener uses the same way, and recruiter capacity that can flex with demand. The scorecard is the piece most teams skip, and it is the piece that keeps quality consistent when ten people are screening instead of one.

This guide walks through each part with templates you can adapt.

## What does a high-volume hiring funnel look like?

High-volume roles, such as customer support, sales development, operations, healthcare support or warehouse supervision, share a pattern: many applicants, a clear set of must-have skills and a need to move fast. The funnel should be built for speed at the top and rigor in the middle.

| Stage | Goal | Typical owner | Keep it fast by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Sourcing | Fill the top of the funnel | Sourcer or RPO team | Using proven channels per role |
| 2. Application review | Remove clear mismatches | Screener | Applying 3 to 5 knockout criteria |
| 3. Structured phone screen | Test must-have skills | Screener | Using a fixed scorecard |
| 4. Skills assessment | Verify job-specific ability | Automated or screener | Short, role-relevant tasks |
| 5. Hiring manager interview | Judge fit and potential | Hiring manager | Pre-booked interview blocks |
| 6. Offer and onboarding | Close and start | Recruiter and HR | Pre-approved offer ranges |

Notice that only stage 5 needs your hiring manager. Everything before it can be run by trained screeners following your criteria.

## How do you write knockout criteria?

Knockout criteria are the non-negotiables that remove a candidate before any conversation. Keep them few and objective:

- Legal authorization to work in the role's location
- Required license or certification, where the role needs one
- Ability to work the required shift or schedule
- Minimum language requirement for customer-facing roles
- Location or commute requirement for on-site roles

Avoid turning preferences into knockouts. "Three years of experience" often screens out strong candidates who learned faster. Test the skill instead.

## How do you build a screening scorecard?

A scorecard turns "I liked them" into evidence. Build one per role family with four to six criteria.

### Step 1: Choose the criteria

Ask the hiring manager: "What does someone need to do well in the first 90 days?" Translate each answer into a criterion. For a customer support role, that might be written communication, problem solving, tool familiarity, schedule reliability and composure with difficult customers.

### Step 2: Write one or two questions per criterion

Use behavioral questions that ask for a real example:

- "Tell me about a time a customer was upset. What did you do, and what happened?"
- "Walk me through how you would look up an order in a system you have not used before."

### Step 3: Define every rating level

This is where most scorecards fail. A 1-to-4 scale with no definitions produces inconsistent ratings. Define each level in plain words.

| Rating | Definition for "Composure with difficult customers" |
| --- | --- |
| 4 - Strong | Gives a specific example, describes calming the customer, solving the issue and following up |
| 3 - Meets | Gives a specific example and a reasonable resolution |
| 2 - Partial | Example is vague, or resolution relied entirely on escalation |
| 1 - Does not meet | No example, or describes arguing with or ignoring the customer |

### Step 4: Set the pass rule

For example: no rating of 1 on any criterion, and an average of 3 or higher. Write the rule down so every screener applies it the same way.

## How do you keep screeners consistent?

Consistency is a process, not a hope.

1. **Calibrate before launch.** Have every screener rate the same two or three recorded or written sample answers and compare.
2. **Review a sample weekly.** A lead reviews a handful of scorecards per screener and discusses any disagreements.
3. **Track pass rates by screener.** A screener who passes far more or fewer candidates than peers needs recalibration.
4. **Close the loop with hiring managers.** If managers keep rejecting candidates who passed the screen, the scorecard needs updating.
5. **Log notes in the ATS.** Notes should quote what the candidate said, not just the rating.

## How do you scale recruiter capacity?

Volume hiring is rarely steady. A seasonal ramp, a new location or a funded growth plan can multiply demand quickly. Your options:

- **Overtime for existing recruiters:** fast, but leads to burnout and slower screening.
- **Temporary contract recruiters:** flexible, but each one needs onboarding and calibration.
- **An RPO partner:** recruiter capacity that ramps up or down with your hiring plan, trained on your scorecards.

With RPO, the scorecard becomes the contract. The partner's screeners follow your criteria and pass rules, and your team reviews quality through the same weekly sample. ## Which funnel metrics should you track?

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Stage-to-stage conversion | Where candidates drop out, and whether criteria are too loose or too strict |
| Days in each stage | Where the process is slow |
| Source quality | Which channels produce candidates who pass screening |
| Screen-to-offer ratio | Whether screening predicts hiring manager decisions |
| Offer acceptance rate | Whether your offer and process are competitive |
| Early retention | Whether the people you hire stay and perform |

Speed problems often sit in scheduling rather than screening. If candidates wait days between stages, see our guide to [how recruiting coordinators reduce time-to-hire](/blog/offshore-recruiting-coordinators-reduce-time-to-hire).

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: an e-commerce company needs to add a large group of seasonal support agents in six weeks. It writes a five-criterion scorecard with defined rating levels, calibrates four outsourced screeners on sample answers and sets a pass rule of no 1s and an average of 3. Hiring managers interview only candidates who pass, and a weekly review compares screener pass rates. When managers start rejecting candidates on typing speed, the team adds a short typing assessment as stage 4.

## Key takeaways

- Build the funnel for speed at the top and rigor in the middle.
- Keep knockout criteria few, objective and tied to the job.
- Define every rating level on your scorecard in plain words, and write down the pass rule.
- Calibrate screeners before launch and review a sample of their work every week.
- Scale with an RPO partner that screens against your scorecard, not its own.
