Offshore document review can be a reliable way to add review capacity, provided it runs on four controls: a written review protocol, calibration before review starts, ongoing quality-control sampling and attorney review of privilege and key documents. Confidentiality depends on keeping documents inside a secure review platform with named, matter-limited access. The reviewers prepare and code; the firm's licensed attorneys supervise, decide privilege and remain responsible for the production.

> This article is general information for law firms and legal departments, not legal advice. Offshore legal support always works under the supervision of the client's licensed attorneys.

## Where does offshore support fit in eDiscovery?

eDiscovery runs through several phases, from identifying and preserving data to producing it. Outsourced legal assistants are most useful in the labor-intensive middle phases.

| eDiscovery phase | Offshore support role | Attorney role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identification and preservation | Track custodians and hold notices | Decide scope, issue holds |
| Collection and processing | Coordinate with vendors, track status | Approve collection scope |
| First-level review | Code responsiveness, issues, potential privilege | Write protocol, answer questions |
| Quality control | Run sampling, track error rates | Review results, adjust protocol |
| Privilege review and log | Draft privilege log entries | Make final privilege calls |
| Production | Prepare production sets and indexes | Approve production |
| Case use | Build chronologies and key document binders | Use them in strategy |

## How do you write a review protocol?

The protocol is the most important document in any review. Reviewers can only be as consistent as the instructions they follow. A strong protocol includes:

- **Case background** in plain language: parties, claims, key dates
- **Responsiveness criteria** tied to the requests for production
- **Issue tags** with a definition and an example for each
- **Privilege indicators,** including attorney names, law firm domains and privilege terms to flag
- **Confidentiality designations** under any protective order
- **Handling rules** for families, attachments, foreign language and technical files
- **Escalation rules** for anything unclear, routed to a question log
- **Coding examples** drawn from the actual document set

Update the protocol whenever the supervising attorney answers a question that affects other documents, and tell every reviewer about the change.

## How do you keep review quality high?

### Calibrate before full review

Have every reviewer code the same small batch of documents. The supervising attorney compares the results, discusses disagreements and updates the protocol. Start full review only once coding is consistent.

### Sample continuously

Quality control should run throughout the review, not just at the end.

1. **Random sampling.** A second reviewer or attorney checks a random sample of each reviewer's coded documents.
2. **Targeted sampling.** Extra checks on high-risk categories, such as documents coded not privileged that contain attorney names.
3. **Error tracking.** Record error types by reviewer and by issue tag.
4. **Feedback loop.** Share findings with reviewers and update the protocol.

### Use a question log

Every reviewer question goes into one shared log, with the attorney's answer. This keeps decisions consistent across the team and creates a record of how the protocol was applied.

### Second-level review

Attorneys review all documents flagged as potentially privileged, all key or "hot" documents and a sample of everything else before production.

### QC checklist

| Control | Frequency | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protocol updated with new decisions | As questions are answered | Supervising attorney |
| Random QC sample | Daily or per batch | Review lead and attorney |
| Targeted privilege screen | Before production | Review lead |
| Reviewer error report | Weekly | Review lead |
| Privilege and key document review | Before production | Licensed attorneys |
| Production approval | Each production | Responsible attorney |

## How do you protect confidentiality?

Confidentiality controls should be in place before the first document is shared.

- **Conflicts.** The firm runs its conflict check before involving any outside team.
- **Agreements.** NDAs and confidentiality terms signed before access is granted, and protective order obligations communicated.
- **Platform-only review.** Reviewers work inside your review platform; documents are not downloaded or emailed.
- **Named, matter-limited accounts.** Each reviewer has their own login with access only to the assigned matter.
- **Two-factor authentication** on the review platform.
- **Audit logs** enabled, so the firm can see who viewed and coded what.
- **Offboarding.** Access removed at the end of the matter or when a reviewer leaves the project.
- **Data residency check.** Confirm with the client and review any protective order or client guidelines about where data may be accessed from.

## When should you use offshore review support?

It tends to fit well when:

- A production deadline is approaching and the document set is large
- Your associates are spending significant time on first-pass review
- You need consistent coverage across U.S. hours, or overnight progress before a morning deadline
- You already use a review platform and want to add capacity inside it

It is a weaker fit when the matter involves restrictions on offshore data access, or when nearly every document requires nuanced legal judgment.

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a litigation team faces a production deadline on a large email set. The supervising associate writes a protocol, runs a calibration batch with three outsourced reviewers and answers questions in a shared log. A review lead samples each reviewer's work daily. All potentially privileged documents go to the associate for final calls, and the partner approves the production. The associates spend their time on privilege and key documents instead of first-pass coding.

## Key takeaways

- A written protocol, calibration, QC sampling and attorney second-level review are the core quality controls.
- Use a single question log so decisions stay consistent across reviewers.
- Keep documents inside a secure review platform with named, matter-limited accounts and audit logs.
- Outsourced reviewers flag potential privilege; licensed attorneys make the final call.
- Check client guidelines and protective orders for any restrictions on where data may be accessed.
