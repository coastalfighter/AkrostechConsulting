An efficient month-end close is a written calendar with clear owners: transactions are cut off, every balance sheet account is reconciled, accruals and adjustments are recorded with support, and management reviews the statements before they are final. An offshore accounting team can prepare almost all of it, and when the team works your U.S. hours, the close moves through review without overnight delays. Your controller or owner keeps the approvals.

Below is a step-by-step close, who should own each step, and how to set it up.

## Why does the close take so long?

Most slow closes share the same causes:

- **Transactions pile up.** If categorization only happens at month-end, the first week is spent catching up.
- **Questions arrive late.** Unknown transactions are discovered on day three, and the answers take days.
- **No calendar.** Everyone knows the close happens, but nobody knows exactly what is due when.
- **Inconsistent reconciliations.** Each account is reconciled differently, so reviews take longer.
- **One person bottleneck.** The controller prepares and reviews everything.

An offshore team fixes the capacity problem. A calendar and templates fix the rest.

## The month-end close checklist

This checklist uses business days after month-end, shown as Day 1, Day 2 and so on. Adjust the timing to your own reporting deadlines.

### Before month-end: keep the books current

- Categorize bank and card transactions weekly
- Record bills and customer invoices as they arrive
- Send a weekly list of open questions to the owner or controller
- Confirm any known one-off items expected this month

### Day 1: cut-off and data collection

- Confirm all bank and card feeds have imported through month-end
- Record any remaining bills and invoices dated in the month
- Collect payroll reports from your payroll provider
- Pull statements for loans and any accounts without feeds

### Day 2: reconciliations

- Reconcile every bank and credit card account to statements
- Reconcile loan balances and interest
- Reconcile payroll liabilities to payroll reports
- Reconcile clearing accounts, such as payment processor balances, to zero or an explained balance

### Day 3: receivables, payables and accruals

- Review AR aging; flag overdue invoices and potential bad debts for management
- Review AP aging; confirm all bills received are recorded
- Prepare accruals for expenses incurred but not yet billed
- Record prepaid expense amortization and depreciation from schedules
- Review deferred revenue, if applicable

### Day 4: review and adjustments

- Prepare the trial balance
- Run a variance analysis against last month and budget
- Draft adjusting journal entries with support attached
- Controller or owner reviews and approves entries

### Day 5: reporting

- Produce P&L, balance sheet and cash-flow summary
- Write short notes explaining significant variances
- Management reviews and signs off
- Lock the period in your accounting software

## Who owns each step?

The table shows a common split between an offshore accounting team and your in-house finance lead.

| Close step | Offshore team prepares | In-house lead reviews or approves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transaction categorization | Yes | Answers question list |
| Bank and card reconciliations | Yes | Spot-checks, reviews exceptions |
| Payroll liability reconciliation | Yes | Reviews |
| AR and AP aging review | Yes, with flags | Decides on write-offs and disputes |
| Accrual and prepaid schedules | Yes | Approves estimates |
| Adjusting journal entries | Drafts with support | Approves before posting |
| Variance analysis | Yes | Reviews explanations |
| Financial statements | Drafts | Final review and sign-off |
| Period lock | Executes after approval | Authorizes |

The principle is the same throughout: the offshore team does the preparation and documentation; a designated person on your side makes judgment calls and approves.

## How do you set up an offshore close team?

1. **Write the close calendar.** Every task, owner and due date in a shared document or project tool.
2. **Standardize reconciliation templates.** Same format for every account: statement balance, book balance, reconciling items, preparer, reviewer, date.
3. **Keep a supporting-documents folder per month.** Statements, schedules and approvals in one place.
4. **Agree on materiality for questions.** For example, the team flags any unexplained item above a threshold you set.
5. **Use named, limited-permission users** in QuickBooks, Xero or Zoho Books, and keep bank logins and payment release in-house. Our [offshore bookkeeping checklist](/blog/offshore-bookkeeping-checklist-data-security) covers the security setup.
6. **Hold a short close review call** after each close to fix what slowed you down.

## What does time-zone alignment change?

A close is a sequence of handoffs. When your accountant works your hours, a question raised at 10 a.m. can be answered by lunch, and a journal entry submitted for approval can be posted the same afternoon. Akrostech accountants work EST, CST, MST or PST, so the review loop runs in the same working day rather than across two.

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: the controller of a services company is preparing and reviewing the close alone. She brings on an offshore accountant, moves categorization to a weekly rhythm and writes a five-day close calendar. The accountant prepares reconciliations and accrual schedules by Day 3, and she spends Day 4 reviewing rather than preparing. Open questions now arrive in a weekly list before month-end, so fewer surprises appear during the close.

## Where does your CPA fit?

Your CPA usually works on annual and tax matters rather than the monthly close. Keep them informed of significant adjustments and give them clean, reconciled books at year-end. Tax positions and filings are decisions for your CPA, not your close team.

## Key takeaways

- Keep the books current weekly so the close starts from a clean base.
- Use a written close calendar with owners and due dates for every task.
- Let the offshore team prepare reconciliations, schedules, entries and drafts; keep approvals in-house.
- Standardize reconciliation templates and support folders to make review fast.
- Choose a team that works your U.S. hours so review loops close the same day.
