Managing a remote virtual assistant well comes down to four things: aligned working hours, secure named access to your tools, written SOPs for every recurring task and a small set of KPIs you review every week. Get those right and distance stops mattering. Skip them and even a talented assistant will feel like extra work.

This playbook covers each piece in the order you should set it up.

## Should your VA work your hours or theirs?

Your hours, for most roles. The biggest hidden cost in offshore work is waiting: a question sent at 10 a.m. that gets answered the next morning. When your VA works your time zone, requests move at the same speed as they would with someone down the hall.

Akrostech VAs are based in India but work EST, CST, MST or PST, depending on what you choose. If your team spans several U.S. time zones, pick the zone where most requests originate and agree on a cut-off time for same-day work.

There is one exception. Some tasks benefit from overnight work, such as preparing a morning briefing or processing a backlog of data entry. If you want that, schedule it deliberately and keep a clear handoff note so nothing is lost between shifts.

## How do you set up secure access?

Set up access on day one, before any real work begins. The goal is that your VA can do everything they need and nothing they do not.

1. **Create a company email account** for the VA. All tool invitations go to that address.
2. **Use named accounts in every tool.** Never share your own login. Named accounts give you an audit trail.
3. **Apply least privilege.** In a CRM, that might mean edit rights on contacts but no export or delete rights.
4. **Share unavoidable credentials through a password manager** so you can revoke them in one click.
5. **Require two-factor authentication** on every account.
6. **Keep sensitive actions behind approval.** Payments, refunds above a threshold and contract signatures should need your sign-off.
7. **Write an offboarding checklist now.** List every account so access can be removed quickly if the engagement changes.

Every Akrostech engagement starts with an NDA, and access is limited to the assigned professional. Your own access hygiene is still the strongest control you have.

| Tool type | Typical VA access | Keep restricted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Delegate access or shared inbox | Your personal admin settings |
| Calendar | Edit events and invites | Account-level sharing settings |
| CRM | Create and edit records, log activity | Bulk export, bulk delete, admin |
| Help desk | Respond to and tag tickets | Billing and workspace settings |
| Accounting | Upload receipts, draft invoices | Approve payments, bank connections |
| Cloud storage | Specific shared folders | Full drive ownership |

## How do you write SOPs a VA will actually follow?

The fastest SOP is a recording. Do the task once while recording your screen and talking through your choices. Then ask your VA to turn the recording into a written checklist. That gives you documentation and proof of understanding in one step.

A good SOP answers five questions:

- **Trigger:** What starts this task? A new email, a form submission, a weekly date?
- **Steps:** What happens, in order, in which tool?
- **Rules:** What decisions are allowed without asking? What thresholds need escalation?
- **Done:** What does a finished task look like, in one sentence?
- **Exceptions:** What are the common edge cases, and who handles them?

Store SOPs in one shared place and ask your VA to update them whenever the process changes. Akrostech keeps each client's SOPs in a secure, client-owned playbook so that work stays consistent through holidays and team changes.

## What daily and weekly rhythm works best?

Structure beats constant check-ins. A simple operating rhythm keeps you informed without micromanaging.

**Daily**

- A start-of-shift check of priorities, usually a short message, not a meeting.
- One channel for urgent requests and one for everything else.
- An end-of-shift summary: what was done, what is blocked, what is next.

**Weekly**

- A 15-minute review of KPIs, open questions and next week's priorities.
- One new task or improvement agreed, so scope grows steadily.

**Monthly**

- A review of SOPs that changed and tasks that could move to your VA next.

## Which KPIs show whether delegation is working?

Pick three or four. More than that and the reporting becomes its own job.

| KPI | What it tells you | How to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turnaround time | Whether requests move quickly | Time from request to completion |
| Accuracy or rework rate | Whether output meets the standard | Tasks returned for correction |
| Backlog size | Whether capacity matches demand | Open tasks at end of week |
| Tasks running without review | How much you have truly handed off | Count of SOPs on spot-check only |
| Response time (support roles) | Customer experience | First response time in help desk |

Set a target for each one after the first two weeks, once you know the baseline. Targets set before you have data tend to be arbitrary.

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a sales leader in Denver works with a VA on MST hours. They track three KPIs: same-day completion of CRM updates, rework on proposal drafts and the number of tasks on spot-check only. After a month, CRM updates are consistently same-day and proposals rarely come back, so the leader moves two more tasks into the VA's SOP library.

## What are the most common management mistakes?

- **Vague requests.** "Clean up the CRM" means nothing. "Merge duplicates in the Leads view and fill missing phone numbers" is a task.
- **No escalation rule.** Without one, your VA will either interrupt you constantly or guess.
- **Reviewing everything forever.** Move tasks to spot-check once they are stable.
- **Sharing passwords in chat.** Use a password manager and named accounts.
- **Skipping the weekly review.** It is the cheapest fifteen minutes in your calendar.

## Key takeaways

- Have your VA work your U.S. time zone to remove waiting time.
- Set up named, least-privilege access with two-factor authentication on day one.
- Turn screen recordings into written SOPs with clear triggers, rules and escalation points.
- Use a daily summary and a short weekly review instead of constant check-ins.
- Track three or four KPIs and set targets only after you have a baseline.
