When a founder asks whether an offshore specialist is worth it, the comparison usually starts and ends with salary. That is the least useful number in the decision. A hire costs what it takes to find someone, employ them, equip them, manage them and replace them when they leave, and most of those costs never appear on the offer letter.

This guide sets out every line that belongs in the comparison, then walks through a worked example you can adapt to your own role.

## The cost of a local hire, line by line

Start with the salary, then add everything the salary does not include.

- **Employer payroll taxes.** In the U.S. the employer pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare on top of wages, plus federal and state unemployment insurance.
- **Benefits.** Health insurance is the largest item for most small employers, followed by retirement contributions and paid time off.
- **Recruiting.** Job board fees, an agency fee if you use one (often a meaningful percentage of first-year salary), and the hours your team spends screening and interviewing.
- **Equipment and software.** A laptop, licences for every tool the role touches, and a share of office space if you have one.
- **Onboarding time.** The weeks before a new hire is fully productive, plus the time of whoever trains them.
- **Management.** The hours a manager spends each week reviewing work and unblocking problems.
- **Turnover.** If the person leaves after a year, you pay most of the recruiting and onboarding cost again.

## The cost of an offshore specialist

With a managed offshore model, most of those lines collapse into one monthly fee. At Akrostech that fee covers the specialist, their equipment, their team lead, quality checks and replacement from a vetted bench if they move on. What remains on your side:

- **The monthly fee**, confirmed after the discovery call.
- **Software seats** for the tools the specialist works in, the same as for any team member.
- **Your time** to explain the work in the first week and review it each week after. A good partner turns the first part into written procedures so it only happens once.

There are no payroll taxes, benefits, placement fees or office costs on your side, because the specialist is not your employee.

## A worked example

Here is a simple model for an operations or executive assistant role. Replace every assumption with your own numbers.

| Line | Local hire (assumed) | Offshore specialist (assumed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base salary or fee | $55,000 a year | One monthly fee |
| Payroll taxes at 7.65% plus unemployment | about $4,600 | none |
| Benefits | $8,000 to $15,000 | none |
| Recruiting | $5,000 or more | none |
| Equipment and space | $2,000 to $6,000 | included |
| Replacement if they leave | repeat recruiting and onboarding | included |

Even before management time, the local hire costs well above its salary. That fully loaded figure, not the salary, is what to compare against an offshore fee.

## What the spreadsheet misses

Cost is half the comparison. The other half is whether the work gets done well, and three things decide that.

### Overlap with your working day

An offshore team that works its own daytime answers your questions tomorrow. That delay compounds: a one-day turnaround on every question turns a week of work into two. Specialists who work your U.S. hours remove the delay, which is why every Akrostech professional works a fixed shift on Eastern, Central, Mountain or Pacific time.

### Quality control

A freelancer working alone has nobody checking their output before it reaches you. A managed model puts a team lead between the work and your inbox. Ask any provider who reviews the work, how often, and what they measure.

### Continuity

People leave. The question is whether the knowledge leaves with them. When every recurring task is written into a standard operating procedure that you own, a replacement can pick it up in days rather than months.

## When a local hire is the better choice

Offshore support is not the answer to every role. Keep a role local when:

- it needs a physical presence: reception, facilities, field work, in-person sales;
- it carries legal authority that must sit with a licensed U.S. professional, such as signing financial statements or giving legal advice;
- it is a senior leadership role whose main job is building relationships inside your company.

For everything else, especially recurring, process-driven work, compare the fully loaded cost and the quality controls, then decide.

## How to run the comparison yourself

1. List the tasks the role would do each week and the hours each takes.
2. Price a local hire using the fully loaded lines above, with your real benefits and recruiting costs.
3. Ask an offshore provider for a monthly fee for the same scope, and for what it includes.
4. Compare the two, then compare the quality controls and the overlap with your hours.
