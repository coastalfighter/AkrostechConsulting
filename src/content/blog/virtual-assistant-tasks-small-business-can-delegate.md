A virtual assistant can take over most of the repeatable work that keeps a small business running: inbox and calendar management, CRM and data entry, customer support, scheduling, research, social media and basic bookkeeping prep. The best tasks to delegate first are the ones that happen often, follow a pattern and have a clear definition of "done."

Below is a working list of 30 tasks, grouped by function, plus a simple way to decide what to hand off and what to keep.

## How do you decide which tasks to delegate?

Before looking at any list, run a quick filter on your own week. A task is a strong candidate for a virtual assistant when it passes most of these tests:

- **It repeats.** Daily or weekly work pays back the time you spend documenting it.
- **It follows rules.** If you can describe the steps in a checklist, someone else can follow them.
- **The output is checkable.** You can tell at a glance whether it was done right.
- **It does not need your authority.** No signatures, no final pricing calls, no hiring decisions.
- **It pulls you out of focused work.** Interruptions cost more than the minutes on the clock.

A useful exercise: log everything you do for five working days, then highlight each item that passes three or more of those tests. That highlighted list is your first VA job description.

## Administrative and executive support

This is where most owners start, because the work is constant and the payoff is immediate.

1. **Inbox triage.** Sort, label, archive and flag the messages that need you, with a short daily summary.
2. **Calendar management.** Schedule meetings, resolve conflicts, add video links and prep notes.
3. **Travel booking.** Flights, hotels and itineraries based on your stated preferences.
4. **Document formatting.** Turn rough drafts into clean proposals, decks and reports.
5. **Meeting notes and follow-ups.** Capture action items and send recap emails.
6. **File organization.** Keep shared drives and folders named and structured consistently.
7. **Expense receipt collection.** Gather receipts and match them to card statements for your bookkeeper.

## Sales and CRM support

Sales teams lose a surprising amount of selling time to data hygiene. A VA can keep the pipeline clean so your salespeople can sell.

8. **CRM updates.** Log calls, update deal stages and add notes in HubSpot, Salesforce or your CRM of choice.
9. **Lead research.** Build prospect lists with company details and verified contact information.
10. **List cleanup.** Remove duplicates, fix formatting and fill gaps in contact records.
11. **Proposal preparation.** Populate templates with client details, pricing you have approved and timelines.
12. **Follow-up reminders.** Track open quotes and nudge the right person when one goes quiet.
13. **Appointment setting.** Book discovery calls from inbound leads into your calendar.

## Customer support

A dedicated VA who works your business hours can become the steady first line of support.

14. **Email and help-desk tickets.** Answer common questions using approved responses.
15. **Live chat coverage.** Staff your website chat during U.S. hours.
16. **Order status and returns.** Look up orders and process routine returns within your policy.
17. **Knowledge base upkeep.** Turn repeated questions into help articles.
18. **Review monitoring.** Track new reviews and draft responses for your approval.

## Marketing and content

19. **Social media scheduling.** Queue posts, track engagement and report what worked.
20. **Community moderation.** Respond to comments and flag anything sensitive.
21. **Blog and newsletter drafts.** First drafts from your outline, ready for your edit.
22. **Product descriptions.** Write and update listings in a consistent format.
23. **Basic graphics.** Resize and adapt templates in tools like Canva.
24. **Competitor tracking.** A monthly snapshot of competitor offers, pricing pages and content.

## Operations and finance support

25. **Data entry.** Move information between forms, spreadsheets and systems accurately.
26. **Invoice preparation.** Draft invoices for your review and track what has been sent.
27. **Vendor coordination.** Request quotes, chase deliveries and keep vendor contacts current.
28. **Reporting.** Pull weekly numbers from your tools into a simple dashboard.
29. **SOP documentation.** Turn your screen recordings into written procedures.
30. **Recruiting admin.** Post jobs, schedule interviews and send candidate updates.

For heavier finance or hiring needs, a specialist is usually a better fit than a generalist. Bookkeeping and reconciliations belong with an [accounting assistant](/services/accounting), and full-cycle hiring support is the job of a [recruitment team](/services/recruitment).

## What should you keep for yourself?

Delegation works best when the boundaries are clear. This table is a simple starting point.

| Task type | Delegate to a VA | Keep in-house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduling and inbox | Triage, booking, reminders | Deciding which meetings matter |
| Sales | CRM entry, research, follow-ups | Pricing, negotiation, closing |
| Customer support | Routine inquiries and tickets | Refund exceptions, escalations |
| Finance | Receipt collection, invoice drafts | Payment approvals, tax decisions |
| Hiring | Posting jobs, scheduling | Interviews that decide the hire, offers |
| Marketing | Drafts, scheduling, reporting | Brand positioning, final approval |

The pattern is simple: your VA prepares, organizes and executes; you decide and approve.

## How should you hand off your first tasks?

Do not hand off all 30 tasks in week one. Pick three to five, then follow a short routine:

1. **Record yourself doing the task once** and talk through your decisions.
2. **Ask your VA to write the checklist** from the recording. This confirms they understood it.
3. **Review the first few outputs** closely, then move to spot checks.
4. **Agree on a daily summary** so you can see what was done without asking.

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a founder of a small services firm logs her week and finds that inbox triage, meeting scheduling, CRM notes and proposal formatting take up a large share of her time. She hands those four tasks to a VA in week one, adds customer email support in week three and social scheduling in week five. Each new task starts only after the previous one is running smoothly without her review.

## Key takeaways

- Delegate tasks that repeat, follow rules and have a checkable result.
- Start with three to five tasks, usually inbox, calendar and CRM work.
- Keep decisions, approvals and anything requiring your signature in-house.
- Use specialists for bookkeeping and full-cycle recruiting.
- Expand the VA's scope one task at a time once the basics run without you.
