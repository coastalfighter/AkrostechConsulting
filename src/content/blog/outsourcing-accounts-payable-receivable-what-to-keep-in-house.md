You can outsource most of the work in accounts payable and accounts receivable, including bill intake, coding, invoicing, payment application, aging reports and routine follow-ups, while keeping the decisions and the money with your team. The rule of thumb is simple: the outsourced team records and prepares; your people approve, release payments and make judgment calls on credit, disputes and write-offs.

This guide lays out exactly where to draw that line.

## Why outsource AP and AR?

AP and AR are high-volume, rules-based and deadline-driven. When they slip, the costs show up quickly: missed early-payment terms, late fees, duplicate payments and invoices that never get followed up. A dedicated offshore accountant can keep both processes moving every day, and the bookkeeping benefits too, because the ledger stays current. ## Accounts payable: what to hand off

A typical AP process has seven steps. Most of them can be run by an outsourced team.

| AP step | Outsourced team | Your team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bill intake from email or portal | Runs it | Forwards bills to one AP inbox |
| Coding to accounts and classes | Runs it | Sets coding rules |
| Matching to purchase orders or receipts | Runs it | Confirms goods or services received when needed |
| Vendor setup and W-9 collection | Prepares | Approves new vendors |
| Due-date tracking | Runs it | Sees the schedule |
| Payment run preparation | Prepares | Approves the run |
| Payment release | Does not do this | Releases payments |
| Vendor statement reconciliation | Runs it | Reviews exceptions |

### Controls to keep in AP

- **Payment release stays in-house.** The person who releases payments should not be the person who records bills.
- **Vendor bank changes are verified by phone,** using a number already on file, never the number in the change request.
- **New vendors are approved** before their first payment.
- **Approval thresholds** are written down, for example, bills above a set amount need a second approver.
- **Duplicate checks** are part of every payment run preparation.

## Accounts receivable: what to hand off

| AR step | Outsourced team | Your team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invoice creation and sending | Runs it | Confirms billable work or shipments |
| Recurring billing | Runs it | Approves changes to plans or pricing |
| Payment application | Runs it | Reviews unapplied cash |
| Aging reports | Runs it | Reviews weekly |
| Scripted payment reminders | Runs it | Approves templates and cadence |
| Customer disputes | Logs and routes | Resolves |
| Credit terms and limits | Does not decide | Decides |
| Write-offs | Prepares support | Approves |

### A reminder cadence you can adapt

An outsourced team works best with a written cadence and approved templates. One common pattern:

1. **Invoice sent** with clear terms and payment options.
2. **Friendly reminder** a few days before the due date.
3. **Due-date notice** on the day payment is due.
4. **First overdue reminder** about a week after the due date.
5. **Second overdue reminder** about two weeks after, copying your account owner.
6. **Escalation** to your team for a personal call or next steps.

Steps 1 through 5 can be handled by the outsourced team. Step 6 belongs to someone who owns the customer relationship.

## What should always stay in-house?

Regardless of how much you outsource, keep these with your team:

- **Releasing payments and access to online banking**
- **Approving new vendors and changes to vendor bank details**
- **Credit decisions** for new and existing customers
- **Resolving customer disputes** and deciding on credits or refunds
- **Write-offs** of uncollectible balances
- **Tax questions,** including sales tax and 1099 treatment, which should go to your CPA

The outsourced team can prepare everything needed for these decisions: the aging report, the dispute history, the vendor's W-9, the 1099 schedule. The decision itself stays with you or your CPA.

## How do you set up an outsourced AP and AR workflow?

1. **Create one AP inbox,** such as a shared address that vendors send bills to.
2. **Write coding rules** for your most common vendors and expense types.
3. **Set approval thresholds and approvers** for bills and payment runs.
4. **Approve AR templates** for invoices and reminders, with your brand voice.
5. **Grant named, limited-permission access** in your accounting software. Keep bank logins in-house. Our [offshore bookkeeping checklist](/blog/offshore-bookkeeping-checklist-data-security) covers access in detail.
6. **Agree on a weekly report:** AP due next week, AR aging, unapplied cash and open disputes.
7. **Review monthly** as part of your [month-end close](/blog/month-end-close-process-with-offshore-accounting-team).

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a regional services company has bills arriving in several inboxes and invoices sent whenever someone remembers. It sets up one AP inbox, hands bill coding and payment run preparation to an offshore accountant, and keeps payment release with the CFO. On the AR side, the accountant sends invoices the day work is approved and follows a five-step reminder cadence. Disputes and write-off decisions go to the account owner and CFO each week, with the accountant preparing the history.

## What metrics show the process is working?

- **Bills recorded within a set number of days of receipt**
- **Payments made on time,** and early-payment terms captured where offered
- **Duplicate or erroneous payments caught before release**
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO)** trend over time
- **Share of AR past due,** by aging bucket
- **Unapplied cash** at month-end

## Key takeaways

- Outsource recording and preparation; keep approvals, payment release and judgment calls in-house.
- Verify any vendor bank change by phone using contact details already on file.
- Use written coding rules, approval thresholds and AR templates so the team can work without guessing.
- Give the outsourced team named, limited-permission access and keep bank logins with your staff.
- Send tax questions, including 1099 and sales tax treatment, to your CPA.
