A business website's cost depends mostly on scope: how many unique page templates you need, whether the design is custom or theme-based, which features and integrations it must support, who writes the content and what support you want after launch. Two quotes that look far apart are often pricing different projects. The fastest way to a reliable number is a clear brief that pins down those drivers before anyone quotes.

We do not publish a price list because the range is genuinely wide. Instead, here is how to understand what you are paying for.

## What are the main cost drivers?

| Cost driver | Lower-effort end | Higher-effort end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page templates | A few templates reused across pages | Many unique layouts |
| Design | Adapted theme or design system | Fully custom design with prototypes |
| Content | You supply final copy and images | Copywriting, photography, video |
| Features | Contact forms, blog | Booking, portals, calculators, multilingual |
| Integrations | Email marketing signup | CRM, ERP, payment, inventory sync |
| E-commerce | Small catalog, standard checkout | Large catalog, custom pricing, subscriptions |
| Migration | New site, no legacy content | Many pages to move with SEO redirects |
| Accessibility and performance | Basic checks | Audited to WCAG 2.2 AA and performance targets |
| Testing | A few browsers | Cross-device QA, accessibility and performance audits |
| Post-launch support | Handoff only | Ongoing care plan and roadmap |

### Page templates matter more than page count

A 40-page site built from four templates can cost less than a 12-page site with 12 unique layouts. When you write a brief, list the types of page you need, such as home, service, case study, blog post, landing page and contact, rather than every individual page.

### Custom design vs. a theme

A theme gets you started faster but often brings code you do not need, which can affect speed and flexibility. A custom design fits your brand and conversion goals precisely, and a reusable design system makes future pages cheaper.

### Integrations are where scope grows

Every connection to another system, such as a CRM, a scheduling tool, an inventory system or a payment provider, needs design, development, testing and error handling. List them early, even the "nice to haves."

## What costs do people forget?

- **Content.** Writing, editing and sourcing images often takes longer than the build. If your team is writing copy, plan the time.
- **SEO migration.** Redirecting old URLs, preserving metadata and setting up search console prevents a traffic drop after relaunch.
- **Analytics and tracking.** Conversion events, tag management and consent handling.
- **Accessibility.** Building to WCAG 2.2 AA from the start is far cheaper than retrofitting later.
- **Licenses.** Premium plugins, Shopify apps, fonts, stock photography and headless CMS plans.
- **Hosting.** Choosing hosting that suits your traffic and platform.
- **Internal time.** Reviews, approvals and feedback rounds from your team.

## Fixed-scope project or dedicated developer?

The engagement model changes how you pay, not just how much.

| Model | Best for | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed-scope project | A new site or redesign with a clear brief | Agreed deliverables, timeline and price |
| Dedicated developer | A steady roadmap of pages, features and integrations | A full-time developer on your hours, billed monthly |
| Care plan | Keeping a live site fast, secure and current | Updates, monitoring, backups, security and reporting |

## How do you get an accurate quote?

Write a one- or two-page brief that covers:

1. **Goals.** What the site must achieve, such as leads, bookings or sales.
2. **Audience.** Who visits and what they need to find.
3. **Page types.** The list of templates, not every page.
4. **Features and integrations.** Including the ones you are unsure about.
5. **Content.** Who writes copy and supplies images.
6. **Platform preferences.** Any requirement for WordPress, Shopify or a custom build. Our [Next.js vs. WordPress vs. Shopify comparison](/blog/nextjs-vs-wordpress-vs-shopify-business-website) can help you decide.
7. **Timeline.** Any fixed launch date and why.
8. **Success metrics.** How you will judge the site six months after launch.

Then ask every provider the same questions:

- What exactly is included, and what is not?
- How many design revision rounds are included?
- Who owns the code, design and accounts at the end?
- What testing is done before launch?
- What happens after launch, and what does support include?

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a professional services firm receives three quotes that look very different. When they compare them line by line, they find that one excludes copywriting, another uses a premium theme with limited customization and the third includes a custom design system, SEO migration and a care plan. Once each quote is mapped to the same brief, the gap reflects real differences in scope rather than pricing alone.

## Key takeaways

- Scope drives cost: count templates, features and integrations, not just pages.
- Content, SEO migration, accessibility, licenses and internal time are often left out of early budgets.
- Choose a fixed-scope project for a defined build and a dedicated developer for an ongoing roadmap.
- Budget for a care plan to keep the site fast, secure and current after launch.
- A clear brief and the same questions for every provider make quotes comparable.
