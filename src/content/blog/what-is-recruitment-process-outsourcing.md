Recruitment process outsourcing, or RPO, means handing some or all of your hiring process to an external team that runs it on your behalf. The recruiters work inside your brand, your applicant tracking system and your interview process. You keep the candidates, the data and the decisions.

That is the short answer. The longer one explains why it is different from a staffing agency, and when it makes sense.

## How RPO works

An RPO partner provides recruiting capacity rather than placements. A typical engagement covers some or all of these stages:

- **Sourcing**: finding candidates on LinkedIn, job boards and in your own database.
- **Screening**: reviewing applications and running first-round calls against a scorecard you approve.
- **Coordination**: scheduling interviews, sending reminders and keeping candidates informed.
- **Offers and onboarding**: preparing offer letters and onboarding paperwork.
- **Reporting**: weekly funnel metrics from your ATS, so you can see where hiring slows down.

You can outsource the whole cycle or only the stages that are stuck. Many teams start with sourcing and scheduling, the two that eat the most hours.

## RPO vs. a staffing agency

The two models are often confused, but they work in opposite directions.

| | Staffing agency | RPO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you pay for | A fee per successful placement | Recruiting capacity, usually monthly |
| Who owns the candidates | The agency | You, in your own ATS |
| Whose brand candidates see | The agency’s | Yours |
| Incentive | Fill the role fast | Improve your hiring process over time |
| Best for | Occasional, hard-to-fill roles | Ongoing or high-volume hiring |

Neither is better in general. An agency is efficient when you hire a few specialised roles a year. RPO becomes cheaper and better once you hire regularly, because you stop paying a fee per hire and the process improves with every role.

## What RPO costs

Pricing depends on scope. The common models are:

- **Monthly capacity**: a fixed fee for one or more recruiters working your requisitions. This is how [Akrostech recruitment support](/services/recruitment-process-outsourcing) is priced.
- **Per-hire fee**: a fee for each hire, usually lower than an agency fee because you share the work.
- **Project**: a fixed fee for a defined hiring push, such as opening a new location.

With an offshore RPO team working your U.S. hours, monthly capacity usually costs far less than an in-house recruiter, and less than agency fees once you hire more than a handful of people a year.

## Signs your hiring is ready for RPO

- Your hiring managers spend hours each week scheduling interviews.
- Applicants wait days for a reply and drop out.
- You pay agency fees on roles you could fill from your own pipeline.
- Nobody can tell you your time-to-hire or where candidates drop out.
- Hiring comes in waves, and your team cannot flex with them.

If two or more of these are true, outsourcing part of the process will likely pay for itself.

## What to look for in an RPO partner

### Recruiters who know your roles

Ask for recruiters who have hired for the same kind of role before, and ask to see a sample candidate summary.

### Your tools, your brand

Recruiters should work in your ATS, from your email domain, with your templates. Candidates should experience your company, not an intermediary.

### Scorecards before sourcing

A good partner insists on a written scorecard for each role before searching. It is the single biggest lever on shortlist quality.

### Plain, weekly reporting

You should see volume, conversion and time in stage every week, drawn from your own ATS data.

### Overlap with your hours

Candidates expect quick replies. Recruiters who work your U.S. time zone can schedule a call the same afternoon.

## Getting started

Start with one role or one stage. A one-day intake session is enough to agree the profile, scorecard and interview loop, and a recruiter can be working in your ATS within 48 hours.
