The most common reason founders put off hiring an assistant is not cost. It is not knowing what to hand over. The work feels too tangled up with everything else to separate out.

The trick is to stop thinking about roles and start thinking about tasks. Here are thirty that small businesses hand to a [virtual assistant](/services/virtual-assistance) in the first month, grouped by where they sit in your week.

## Inbox and calendar

1. **Triage the inbox every morning** into reply, read and archive, so you open email to a short list instead of a long one.
2. **Draft routine replies** in your voice for you to approve, then send them directly once the tone is right.
3. **Schedule meetings** across time zones, with buffers you set and no back-and-forth from you.
4. **Prepare meeting briefs**: who you are meeting, the last conversation, and what they want.
5. **Chase follow-ups** from meetings and keep a list of what is waiting on whom.
6. **Book travel** and build an itinerary with confirmations in one place.

## Sales and CRM

7. **Research prospects** against your ideal customer profile and add them to the CRM.
8. **Clean the CRM**: merge duplicates, fill missing fields, close dead deals.
9. **Log calls and emails** to the right records so the pipeline tells the truth.
10. **Send follow-up sequences** after demos and events.
11. **Prepare proposals** from your templates for you to review.
12. **Build lead lists** for campaigns from LinkedIn and industry directories.

## Customers

13. **Answer support tickets** with written macros and a clear escalation path.
14. **Handle order questions**: tracking, returns, exchanges and refunds within limits you set.
15. **Reply to reviews** on Google and marketplaces in a consistent voice.
16. **Run onboarding emails** for new customers and check they have logged in.
17. **Collect testimonials** from happy customers, with their permission.

## Finance admin

18. **Chase unpaid invoices** with a polite, persistent cadence.
19. **Collect receipts** and match them to card transactions for your bookkeeper.
20. **Process expense reports** against your policy.
21. **Track subscriptions** and flag tools nobody is using.

For anything beyond admin, such as reconciliations or month-end close, use a trained [bookkeeper or accountant](/services/accounting-assistance) rather than a general assistant.

## Operations

22. **Write standard operating procedures** for recurring tasks as they take them over.
23. **Coordinate vendors**: quotes, renewals and deliveries.
24. **Manage documents**: naming, filing and access in your shared drive.
25. **Prepare weekly reports** from your tools into one page.
26. **Update your website content** and product listings.
27. **Schedule social posts** from a calendar you approve.
28. **Coordinate hiring logistics**: interview scheduling and candidate updates. For full recruiting, see [recruitment process outsourcing](/services/recruitment-process-outsourcing).
29. **Order supplies** and keep inventory sheets current.
30. **Plan events and team offsites**: venues, bookings and attendee lists.

## How to hand a task over

A list is only useful if the handover works. Use the same four steps for every task.

### Record it once

Next time you do the task, record your screen and talk through what you are doing and why. Ten minutes of video saves an hour of explaining.

### Write the rules

Note the decisions: which emails you always answer yourself, which refunds need your approval, which meetings are never moved. Your assistant turns this into a written procedure that you approve.

### Start supervised

For the first week, your assistant drafts and you approve. Once the drafts stop needing edits, they act directly and you review a summary.

### Review weekly

A short weekly report keeps you informed without micromanaging: what was done, what is waiting on you, and what should change.

## Which tasks to keep

Keep anything that needs your judgment on people, money or strategy: hiring decisions, pricing, negotiations, and conversations with your most important customers. Everything that follows a pattern can move.

## Start small

You do not need to hand over thirty tasks at once. Pick the five that cost you the most time each week, hand those over in week one, and add more as the procedures settle in. Most teams are surprised by how quickly the list grows.
