Legal process outsourcing (LPO) means delegating legal support work, such as research, document review, drafting support, record retrieval and case administration, to an outside team that works under the supervision of your firm's licensed attorneys. It lets attorneys spend their time on judgment, strategy and client relationships while trained support staff handle the preparation. LPO staff do not give legal advice, and the supervising attorney remains responsible for every piece of work product.

This guide covers which tasks are safe to outsource, which are not, and how to set up supervision properly.

## How does LPO work?

In a typical engagement, a dedicated legal assistant, trained in U.S. legal terminology and procedures, works inside your firm's tools during your business hours. Attorneys assign tasks, the assistant prepares the work, and an attorney reviews and approves it before it goes anywhere.

> Important: offshore legal support is not legal advice and is not a substitute for a licensed attorney. Every engagement runs under the supervision of the client's licensed attorneys, who review and take responsibility for all work product.

## Is outsourcing legal work ethical?

Generally yes, with conditions. The ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct, including the rules on supervising nonlawyer assistance and on confidentiality, form the foundation most states build on, and the ABA and many state bars have issued ethics opinions on outsourcing. The common themes:

- **Supervision.** A lawyer must supervise the work and remains responsible for it.
- **Competence.** The firm should be satisfied that the provider is qualified for the tasks assigned.
- **Confidentiality.** Client information must be protected with appropriate safeguards.
- **No unauthorized practice of law.** Nonlawyers cannot give legal advice or exercise independent legal judgment for clients.
- **Client communication and billing.** Some opinions address when to inform clients and how outsourced work may be billed.

Rules and opinions vary by jurisdiction, so review the guidance that applies to your firm. This article is general information, not legal advice.

## Which tasks can law firms safely outsource?

The best candidates are document-heavy, well-defined and easy to review.

| Task | Why it suits outsourcing | Attorney checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal research and memo drafts | Defined question, verifiable sources | Attorney verifies authority and conclusions |
| First-pass document review | Rules-based coding against a protocol | Attorney reviews privilege and hot documents |
| Medical record retrieval and summaries | Structured, time-consuming work | Attorney reviews summary before use |
| Deposition and transcript summaries | Clear format, easy to check | Attorney reviews before relying on it |
| Drafting support from templates | Firm templates set the standard | Attorney edits and approves final draft |
| Immigration and other form preparation | Field-by-field, checklist driven | Attorney reviews before filing |
| Contract abstraction and redlines against a playbook | Playbook defines the rules | Attorney approves deviations |
| Case calendar and deadline tracking | Process-driven | Attorney confirms critical deadlines |
| Client intake and document collection | Scripted, administrative | Attorney decides on representation |
| Bankruptcy petition preparation | Data-heavy, form-based | Attorney reviews and signs |

## Which tasks should stay with your attorneys?

Some work cannot be delegated, and some should not be:

- **Giving legal advice** to clients, in any form
- **Setting legal strategy** or making judgment calls on a matter
- **Signing pleadings, filings and opinion letters**
- **Appearing in court** or at depositions as counsel
- **Final privilege determinations**
- **Negotiating on behalf of clients**
- **Accepting or declining representation**

A useful test: if the task requires exercising legal judgment on behalf of a client, it stays with a licensed attorney. If it prepares information for that judgment, it can usually be delegated with review.

## How do you set up supervision that works?

### Assign a supervising attorney

Each outsourced assistant should report to a named attorney for each matter or practice group. That attorney assigns work, answers questions and approves output.

### Write task instructions

For each recurring task, document the scope, format, sources to use and when to escalate. A research assignment, for example, should state the jurisdiction, the question, the deadline and the expected memo format.

### Build in review points

Decide in advance what the attorney reviews before work is used. For first-pass review, that may be a sample plus all privileged and key documents. For drafts, it is every document before it leaves the firm.

### Control access and confidentiality

- NDAs and confidentiality terms in place before work begins
- Named accounts in your case management and document systems
- Access limited to the matters the assistant is assigned
- Conflict checks run by the firm before any matter is shared

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a small personal injury firm assigns an outsourced legal assistant to request medical records, track outstanding requests and prepare chronological medical summaries. A senior paralegal and the handling attorney review each summary before it is used in a demand letter, which the attorney drafts and signs. The attorneys spend less time on record logistics and more time on case strategy and client calls.

## What should you look for in an LPO provider?

1. **Training in U.S. legal terminology and procedures** for your practice areas
2. **Dedicated staff,** not a rotating pool
3. **Confidentiality controls,** including NDAs and limited access
4. **Willingness to work in your systems,** such as your case management platform
5. **Clear turnaround tracking and review checkpoints**
6. **Time-zone coverage** that matches your needs, including after-hours support

## Key takeaways

- LPO delegates legal support work, not legal judgment, and always runs under attorney supervision.
- Outsourcing is generally permitted with supervision, competence and confidentiality safeguards, but check your jurisdiction's rules.
- Document-heavy, well-defined tasks with clear review points are the best candidates.
- Advice, strategy, signatures, court appearances and final privilege calls stay with attorneys.
- Assign a supervising attorney, write task instructions and define review points before work starts.
