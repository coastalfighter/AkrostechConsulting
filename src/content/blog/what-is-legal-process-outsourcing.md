Legal process outsourcing, or LPO, means sending defined legal support work to an external team that works under the supervision of your attorneys. The team prepares work product; your lawyers review it, rely on it and remain responsible for it.

Done well, LPO lets a firm or legal department handle more matters without adding headcount, and lets attorneys spend their hours on the judgment clients pay for.

## What LPO covers

Most legal work has a core of judgment surrounded by a much larger volume of preparation. LPO takes the preparation:

- **Document preparation** from your templates and precedents
- **Contract management**: abstraction, renewal tracking and clause libraries
- **Legal research summaries** prepared to a written brief, for counsel to verify
- **Litigation support**: first-pass document review, indexing, chronologies and deposition summaries
- **Compliance operations**: entity records, filing calendars and policy registers
- **Cite-checking and formatting** of briefs and memos

## Tasks that are safe to outsource

The test is simple: will a licensed attorney review this before anyone relies on it? If yes, it can usually be outsourced.

| Usually safe to outsource | Keep with your attorneys |
| --- | --- |
| First drafts from approved templates | Legal advice to clients |
| Contract abstraction and summaries | Negotiation strategy |
| Research memos for review | Final review and sign-off |
| First-pass document review | Privilege decisions |
| Calendaring and deadline tracking | Court appearances |
| Exhibit and binder preparation | Anything requiring a licence |

## How supervision works

Professional responsibility rules in the U.S. allow lawyers to use non-lawyer assistance, including outsourced assistance, provided they supervise it appropriately and protect client confidences. In practice that means:

- A named supervising attorney for each workstream.
- Written instructions and templates for each task type.
- Review of work product before it is relied on.
- Starting with low-risk work and widening scope as quality is proven.

A good [LPO partner](/services/legal-process-outsourcing) builds this into the engagement: our legal assistants start on supervised, low-risk tasks for the first two weeks, and quality is reported to the supervising attorney every week.

## Protecting client confidentiality

Confidentiality is the first question every partner asks, and it should be.

- **NDAs** for every person who touches client files, signed before access is granted.
- **Work inside your systems.** Assistants use named accounts in your document management system, such as Clio, NetDocuments, iManage or Relativity, so the record of work stays on your side.
- **Matter-level access**, granted for the matters they work on and removed when those close.
- **No local downloads** unless you explicitly allow them.
- **Conflict checks** handled by your firm in the normal way, since the work is done for your clients under your supervision.

## Where LPO pays off fastest

### Contract backlogs

In-house teams often inherit hundreds of agreements with no record of renewal dates. A short abstraction project produces a register and a renewal calendar, then a simple intake process keeps it current.

### Litigation peaks

Discovery and deposition-heavy phases need many hands for a few weeks. An outsourced team flexes with the matter instead of your payroll.

### Recurring compliance

Entity records, annual reports and filing calendars are precise, repetitive and deadline-driven, which makes them ideal for a trained team with a checklist.

## Working hours matter

Legal work runs on deadlines. A team that works your U.S. hours can take a task at 10 am and return it the same afternoon, and answer questions while your attorneys are still at their desks.

## Getting started

Begin with one task type and one supervising attorney. Agree the template, the review step and the turnaround, then measure error rates and turnaround times for a month before widening the scope.
