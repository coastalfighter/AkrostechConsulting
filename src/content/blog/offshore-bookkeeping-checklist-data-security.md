Outsourcing bookkeeping offshore works when you treat it as a controlled handoff: clean up and document your books first, give the bookkeeper named, least-privilege access to your accounting software, keep payment authority and bank logins with your team, and agree on a written list of monthly deliverables. Security comes from those controls, not from geography.

Use the checklist below to prepare, launch and run the engagement.

## Why do businesses outsource bookkeeping offshore?

The usual reasons are cost, consistency and capacity. A dedicated offshore bookkeeper can keep your books current every week instead of in a scramble before tax season, and an engagement with Akrostech includes a named accountant, a team lead for review and extra capacity at month-end. Our accountants work in QuickBooks, Xero and Zoho Books and are trained on U.S. accounting workflows.

The risk most owners worry about is data. That is a fair concern, and it is manageable with the right setup.

## Phase 1: Prepare your books

Before a bookkeeper touches a transaction, get the basics in one place.

- [ ] **Chart of accounts** exported and reviewed. Remove accounts nobody uses.
- [ ] **Bank and credit card statements** for the last few months.
- [ ] **Open balances.** Lists of unpaid bills and unpaid customer invoices.
- [ ] **Vendor and customer lists** with contact and payment terms.
- [ ] **Payroll setup.** Which provider you use and how payroll posts to the books.
- [ ] **Sales tax setup.** Where you collect, and how it is recorded. Confirm requirements with your CPA.
- [ ] **Categorization rules.** A short note on how you want common transactions coded.
- [ ] **Prior-year adjustments** from your CPA, so opening balances match.
- [ ] **A list of who approves what,** such as bills, refunds and journal entries.

If your books are behind, plan for a clean-up phase first. Akrostech starts every engagement with a books review of about two days, followed by catch-up and reconciliation work if needed.

## Phase 2: Set up secure access

This is the most important part of the checklist. Every item here is within your control.

| Control | What to do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named user accounts | Invite the bookkeeper as their own user in your accounting software | Creates an audit trail of who changed what |
| Least privilege | Grant only the roles needed, such as bookkeeper or accountant role | Limits what can go wrong |
| No bank logins | Connect banks through bank feeds in your software instead | The bookkeeper never holds credentials that move money |
| Payment approval | Keep bill payment and transfers behind your approval | Separates recording from paying |
| Two-factor authentication | Require it on every account | Blocks most credential-based attacks |
| Password manager | Share any unavoidable credentials through one | Revoke in one step |
| Shared document folder | One folder for receipts and statements, with limited sharing | Keeps files out of email |
| NDA | Signed before any data is shared | Sets legal obligations for confidentiality |
| Offboarding list | Write down every account granted | Revoke access quickly if needed |

### The single most important rule

**Separate recording from paying.** Your bookkeeper can record bills, prepare payment runs and reconcile accounts. A person on your team approves and releases payments. This one rule removes the largest category of risk.

## Phase 3: Define the deliverables

Ambiguity causes more problems than anything else. Agree in writing on what you get and when.

**Weekly**

- Categorize bank and card transactions
- Record bills and customer invoices
- Flag unknown transactions with a question list

**Monthly**

- Reconcile all bank, card and loan accounts
- Review accounts receivable and accounts payable aging
- Prepare P&L, balance sheet and cash-flow summary
- Complete a month-end close checklist

**Quarterly and annually**

- Prepare schedules your CPA requests
- Support 1099 preparation and sales tax reconciliation for CPA review
- Hand off a clean, reconciled ledger at year-end

Our walkthrough of the [month-end close with an offshore team](/blog/month-end-close-process-with-offshore-accounting-team) shows how the monthly cadence fits together.

## Phase 4: Run the first 30 days

1. **Week 1:** Books review, access setup and categorization rules confirmed.
2. **Week 2:** Bookkeeper categorizes and reconciles one month; you review every question they raise.
3. **Week 3:** Move to the current month; agree on a weekly question list format.
4. **Week 4:** First full month-end close and report; adjust the checklist based on what you learned.

### An illustrative example

Illustrative example: a small distribution company outsources its bookkeeping. The owner invites the bookkeeper as a named user in QuickBooks Online with a limited role, connects bank feeds so no banking login is shared, and keeps bill payment approval with the office manager. The bookkeeper sends a weekly list of uncategorized transactions, and the owner answers it in a few minutes. At year-end, the CPA receives a reconciled ledger and the schedules they asked for.

## What should stay with your team or your CPA?

- **Tax decisions and filings.** Your CPA advises, signs and files. A bookkeeper prepares, but does not replace tax advice.
- **Payment approvals and bank access.**
- **Decisions about entity structure, owner draws and financing.**
- **Final review of financial statements** before they go to lenders or investors.

For more on dividing the work, see our guide to [outsourcing AP and AR and what to keep in-house](/blog/outsourcing-accounts-payable-receivable-what-to-keep-in-house).

## Key takeaways

- Clean up and document your books before the handoff, or plan for a clean-up phase.
- Use named, least-privilege users in your accounting software and connect banks through feeds.
- Separate recording from paying: your team approves and releases every payment.
- Agree on weekly, monthly and annual deliverables in writing.
- Keep tax decisions with your CPA; your bookkeeper prepares the information they need.
