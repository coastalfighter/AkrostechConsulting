Ask five agencies to quote for a business website and you may get five answers spread across two orders of magnitude. They are rarely quoting for the same thing. The price of a website is driven by a handful of factors, and once you know them you can compare quotes properly and decide where to spend.

## The five factors that drive the price

### 1. Number of unique templates

Price follows the number of distinct page layouts, not the number of pages. Fifty blog posts share one template; a homepage, a service page and a pricing page are three. A small site might have five templates, a full business site fifteen to twenty.

### 2. Design: custom or adapted

Adapting a theme is quick and cheap but looks like everyone else. A custom design starts from your buyers and your brand, and includes a design system so new pages stay consistent. It costs more upfront and usually converts better.

### 3. Content management

Do you need to edit the site yourself? A CMS such as WordPress or a headless CMS like Sanity adds content modelling, editor training and preview workflows. Worth it if you publish often.

### 4. Functionality and integrations

Contact forms are simple. Online stores, customer portals, booking systems, CRM and ERP integrations, and anything with user accounts add significant engineering, testing and security work.

### 5. Migration and SEO protection

Replacing an existing site means mapping every URL that earns traffic, setting redirects and moving content. Skipping it is the most expensive mistake in a redesign, because lost rankings take months to recover.

## Typical price ranges

These are typical starting points for a professionally built site. Your scope sets the actual figure.

| Project | Typical scope | Typical timeline | Starting price at Akrostech |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page or small site | Up to 5 pages | 1–2 weeks | $2,500 |
| Business website | Up to 20 templates with a CMS | 4–6 weeks | $6,500 |
| E-commerce or web application | Store, portal or custom tool | 8–14 weeks | $15,000 |

U.S. agencies often charge two to three times these figures for the same scope. Template marketplaces and DIY builders cost less upfront but rarely include strategy, custom design, performance work or migration.

## Ongoing costs to budget for

A website has running costs after launch:

- **Domain and hosting**: from a few dollars a month on modern static hosting to more for commerce platforms.
- **Platform fees**: Shopify plans, premium plugins or CMS subscriptions.
- **Maintenance**: updates, security patches, backups and monitoring. A care plan bundles these with a block of change hours.
- **Content and improvement**: the pages and experiments that keep the site earning.

## Where to spend and where to save

Spend on the pages that sell: your homepage, service or product pages and your contact flow. Spend on speed, because slow pages lose visitors before they read a word; see our guide to [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-for-business-owners). Spend on migration if you have rankings to protect.

Save by reusing templates, writing your own first drafts of copy, and launching with the pages you need now rather than every page you might want later.

## How to get an accurate quote

1. **List your goals**: what should a visitor do, and how will you measure it?
2. **Inventory your content**: which pages exist, which earn traffic, which are new.
3. **List integrations**: CRM, email, payments, booking, analytics.
4. **Ask for a fixed price after discovery.** A short paid discovery produces a written scope that two vendors can quote against fairly.
