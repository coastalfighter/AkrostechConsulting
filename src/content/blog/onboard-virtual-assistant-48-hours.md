The first two days with a new assistant decide how the next six months go. Plan them and your assistant is closing real tasks by the end of day one. Improvise them and you spend a fortnight answering questions you could have written down once.

Here is the plan we use when an Akrostech [virtual assistant](/services/virtual-assistance) starts with a new client.

## Before day one: one hour of preparation

### Choose the first five tasks

Pick five recurring tasks that take you the most time and need the least judgment. Good first tasks are inbox triage, calendar management, CRM updates, invoice chasing and meeting notes. If you need ideas, see [30 tasks to delegate](/blog/virtual-assistant-tasks-to-delegate).

### Record each task once

The next time you do each task, record your screen and talk through it. Five short videos are worth more than a long document.

### List the access needed

Write down every tool the five tasks touch. That list becomes the access checklist.

## Day one, morning: access and introductions

### Grant access safely

- Create a named account for your assistant in each tool. Never share your own login.
- Use delegated access for email and calendar rather than your password.
- Share anything that needs a credential through a password manager.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere.
- Give the narrowest role that covers the five tasks; widen it later.

### A 30-minute kickoff call

Cover how you like to communicate, what counts as urgent, which people and topics you always handle yourself, and when you want the daily summary.

## Day one, afternoon: the first real work

Start with the inbox. Your assistant labels and triages for the afternoon without replying, then sends you a short summary of what they found. You correct any labels that are wrong. By the end of the first day you have a cleaner inbox and a first draft of your triage rules.

## Day two: procedures and supervised work

### Turn videos into procedures

Your assistant writes a short standard operating procedure for each of the five tasks from your recordings: the trigger, the steps, the decisions and the exceptions. You review each one in a few minutes.

### Supervised drafts

For tasks that send anything in your name, your assistant drafts and you approve. Once a type of draft stops needing edits, move it to "act and report".

### The first daily summary

At the end of day two you receive a short summary: what was done, what is waiting on you, and any questions. This becomes the daily rhythm.

## The weekly rhythm that keeps it working

- **A daily summary** in the format you chose.
- **A weekly report** from the assistant's team lead, covering completed work, open loops and suggestions.
- **A fifteen-minute weekly call** for the first month, then fortnightly.
- **One new task a week**, each with its own procedure, until the assistant owns a whole function.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Handing over judgment before process.** Start with tasks that follow a pattern.
- **Sharing passwords.** It is a security risk and makes offboarding painful.
- **No written procedures.** Without them, every absence or replacement starts from zero.
- **Working different hours.** An assistant in a different working day turns every question into a day's delay. Ours work your U.S. hours for exactly this reason.

## Ready in two days

With an hour of preparation and this plan, most clients have an assistant closing real tasks on day one and owning five of them by the end of the first week.
